## 0:46 — #9 NAN MADOL, MICRONESIA

[TEXT: "#9 — Nan Madol, Pohnpei · c. 1200–1500 CE"]
[MAP: zoom from the Pacific to Pohnpei]

Number nine. Off the coast of Pohnpei, in Micronesia, more than a hundred artificial islets sit in a shallow lagoon.

[REAL: aerial over the islets and canals; close-up of stacked basalt]

Their walls are long, six-sided basalt columns, stacked crosswise like a log cabin and packed with coral. This was the seat of the Saudeleur rulers, and its monumental building is associated especially with about 1200 to 1500 CE.

[3D: P-9A Cutting — "3D Reconstruction" label]

The basalt was quarried elsewhere on Pohnpei, where volcanic rock naturally cools into columns.

[3D: P-9B Building]

Then it was moved, possibly by raft, and stacked, one heavy column at a time.

[3D: P-9C Finished]

Exactly how is uncertain. Rafts, ramps and levers have all been proposed, and no written record settles it. What's clear is the organisation behind it: rulers who could command labour for generations.

Moving stone around one island is hard. Try moving it across a country, five thousand years ago.

## 1:41 — #8 STONEHENGE, ENGLAND

[TEXT: "#8 — Stonehenge, Wiltshire · c. 3000–1500 BCE"]
[REAL: Stonehenge at sunrise, slow orbit]

Number eight: Stonehenge, built in phases over more than a thousand years.

[MAP: Preseli Hills → Salisbury Plain (~150 mi); West Woods → Stonehenge (~20 mi)]

Its stones didn't all travel the same distance. The giant sarsens came from the Marlborough Downs area, largely West Woods, about twenty miles away. The smaller bluestones came from the Preseli Hills in south-west Wales, roughly a hundred and fifty miles away.

[3D: P-8A Cutting — "3D Reconstruction" label]
[3D: P-8B Building]

Archaeologists believe builders used wooden sledges, timber tracks and big teams on ropes. But the exact routes, overland or partly by water, are still debated.

[3D: P-8C Finished]
[REAL: the Altar Stone area]
[TEXT: "Altar Stone — traced to NE Scotland (Nature, 2024)"]

But the strangest part is still coming. In 2024, a study in *Nature* traced the Altar Stone, at the monument's centre, to north-east Scotland, roughly seven hundred kilometres away. How it got here is unknown.

Stonehenge's builders dragged their stones. On one Pacific island, people say the stones walked.

## 2:37 — #7 THE MOAI, RAPA NUI (CHILE)

[TEXT: "#7 — Moai, Rapa Nui, Chile"]
[MAP: Rapa Nui, ~3,500 km off the Chilean coast]

Number seven: the Moai of Rapa Nui, a remote Pacific island that is part of Chile.

[REAL: Ahu Tongariki row; Rano Raraku slopes with half-buried heads]

Nearly a thousand statues were carved here, most from one volcanic quarry, Rano Raraku. Most are a few metres tall, but some are huge. Paro, one of the largest ever erected, is estimated at around eighty tonnes.

[3D: P-7A Cutting — "3D Reconstruction" label]
[3D: P-7B Building]

Rapa Nui tradition says the Moai walked. In 2012, archaeologists Terry Hunt and Carl Lipo tested that with a replica of about five tonnes. Eighteen people, three ropes, and the upright statue rocked forward, step by step. Statues abandoned along ancient roads fit that picture.

[3D: P-7C Finished]

It's a strongly supported explanation, but not proven for every statue, especially the heaviest. And in the quarry lies one giant, around twenty metres long, that was never finished.

On Rapa Nui, the challenge was about a thousand statues. In Egypt, it was over two million blocks.

## 3:34 — #6 THE GREAT PYRAMID OF GIZA, EGYPT

[TEXT: "#6 — Great Pyramid, Giza · c. 2560 BCE"]
[REAL: drone rise over the Giza plateau]

Number six: the Great Pyramid, built for the pharaoh Khufu around 4,500 years ago. It originally stood about 146.6 metres tall, from an estimated 2.3 million blocks, its sides aligned to the cardinal directions with remarkable, though not perfect, accuracy.

[3D: P-6A Cutting — "3D Reconstruction" label]

Here, the evidence is strong. Most core limestone was quarried on the plateau itself. Finer casing stone came from Tura, across the Nile.

[REAL: Wadi al-Jarf papyri, museum footage]
[TEXT: "Diary of Merer — oldest known inscribed papyri"]

Papyri found at Wadi al-Jarf include the logbook of an official named Merer, recording his crew shipping that limestone to Giza by boat.

[3D: P-6B Building]

Egyptian art shows loads dragged on sledges, and a workers' town has been excavated nearby.

[3D: P-6C Finished]

What we don't know is the ramp system. Straight, zigzag, spiralling: each has supporters. None is proven.

But so far, every builder had farming behind them. The next site breaks that rule.

## 4:33 — #5 GÖBEKLI TEPE, TÜRKIYE

[TEXT: "#5 — Göbekli Tepe · c. 9600–8200 BCE"]
[REAL: protective roof over the enclosures; T-pillars with carved animals]

Number five: Göbekli Tepe, in south-eastern Türkiye. Its monumental enclosures date to roughly 9600 to 8200 BCE, around seven thousand years before the Great Pyramid.

Giant T-shaped limestone pillars, some over five metres tall, stand in rings, carved with foxes, boars, snakes and vultures. Some even have arms and hands.

[3D: P-5A Cutting — "3D Reconstruction" label]

The builders were hunter-gatherer communities, before fully established farming in the region. They quarried the pillars with stone tools. One unfinished pillar still lies in the bedrock.

[3D: P-5B Building]
[3D: P-5C Finished]

Why build it? Ritual and communal gathering are the leading interpretations, but the debate is open. Some researchers even suggest gatherings like these helped push people toward farming.

Göbekli Tepe shows ambition. The next site shows precision, and more wild theories than almost anywhere on Earth.

## 5:27 — #4 PUMA PUNKU, BOLIVIA

[TEXT: "#4 — Puma Punku, Tiwanaku · c. 600 CE"]
[MAP: Lake Titicaca, Tiwanaku ~3,850 m]

Number four: Puma Punku, part of the larger Tiwanaku complex near Lake Titicaca, in Bolivia.

[REAL: scattered blocks; macro on interior right angles and the H-shaped blocks]

Its stones now lie toppled. But look closely: flat faces, crisp interior angles, and the famous H-shaped blocks, repeated so consistently they look standardised.

[3D: P-4A Cutting — "3D Reconstruction" label]

It's tempting to jump to lasers. The evidence points elsewhere. The largest slabs are red sandstone, traced to a quarry about ten kilometres away. Andesite came from across the lake. Researchers have found hammerstones and studied tool marks consistent with pecking, grinding and polishing.

[3D: P-4B Building]

Repeated shapes suggest templates and skilled specialists.

[3D: P-4C Finished]

What's uncertain is exactly how such consistent angles were achieved, and how the complex looked before collapse and looting. These stones don't need lost technology. They needed extraordinary skill.

Centuries later, the Inca took fitted stone to an extreme.

## 6:24 — #3 SACSAYHUAMÁN, PERU

[TEXT: "#3 — Sacsayhuamán, Cusco · 15th century CE"]
[REAL: walk along the zigzag walls; close-up of a many-angled joint]

Number three: Sacsayhuamán, the Inca complex above Cusco. Its zigzag walls are built from enormous polygonal stones, the largest estimated at well over a hundred tonnes, fitted without mortar.

[3D: P-3A Cutting — existing prompt — "3D Reconstruction" label]
[3D: P-3B Placing — existing prompt]

One leading explanation for the tight joints is patient repetition: shape a stone, test it against its neighbour, mark where it touches, adjust, and test again. Experiments with hammerstones show how effective that can be.

[3D: P-3C Finished — existing prompt]

How the largest blocks were moved and lifted is still uncertain. And earthquakes? Interlocking walls have helped many Inca structures survive centuries of tremors, but they aren't indestructible. Much of Sacsayhuamán was dismantled by the Spanish to build colonial Cusco. The biggest stones stayed, too heavy to take.

So far, everything went up. Number two goes down.

## 7:18 — #2 DERINKUYU, TÜRKIYE

[TEXT: "#2 — Derinkuyu, Cappadocia"]
[REAL: tunnel descent, POV; round rolling-stone door]

Number two: Derinkuyu, in Cappadocia. Beneath the town lies an underground complex of multiple levels, with sections around sixty metres below ground.

[3D: P-2A Cutaway — existing prompt — "3D Reconstruction" label]

Rooms, stables, storage areas, wells, and ventilation shafts carrying air to the deepest levels. Huge round stone doors could be rolled shut from inside.

[3D: P-2B Going Down — existing prompt]

The rock is soft volcanic tuff, carved by hand. The complex grew over centuries and was greatly expanded in the Byzantine era as shelter in dangerous times.

[3D: P-2C Living — existing prompt]
[TEXT: "20,000 people? — not securely established"]

You'll often hear it held twenty thousand people. That number isn't securely established, and it isn't a proven population living here at once.

Derinkuyu was carved out a basket at a time. Number one went the other way: single stones of almost unbelievable size.

## 8:08 — #1 BAALBEK, LEBANON

[TEXT: "#1 — Baalbek, Lebanon · Roman period"]
[REAL: Temple of Jupiter columns; slow tilt down to the Trilithon with people for scale]

Number one: Baalbek, in Lebanon's Beqaa Valley. Beneath the Roman Temple of Jupiter lies a stone platform. In one course of its wall sit three limestone blocks side by side: the Trilithon. Each is about nineteen metres long, and commonly estimated at around eight hundred tonnes.

[3D: P-1A Cutting — existing prompt — "3D Reconstruction" label]

They were cut about two thousand years ago, from a limestone quarry nearby.

[3D: P-1B Dragging — existing prompt]

Many researchers believe they were moved on timber tracks or rollers by enormous teams, using levers and capstans, giant winches turned by people or animals.

[REAL: the quarry; people standing on the giant unfinished blocks]
[TEXT: "Estimated ~1,000 t" / "Estimated ~1,650 t (exposed 2014)"]

And the strangest part is waiting in that quarry: even larger blocks, never finished. One, the Stone of the Pregnant Woman, is estimated at around a thousand tonnes. In 2014, German archaeologists exposed another, estimated at around sixteen hundred and fifty.

[3D: P-1C Finished — existing prompt]

Modern cranes can lift heavier loads. The real question is how Roman-era engineers did it with rope, timber, muscle and mechanical advantage, and why they left the biggest stones behind.
